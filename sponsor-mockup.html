Sponsorship concepts

Shown at the size they'd sit at in a corner of Today. The four tiers

Round prices, not $X.99 — this is a support-the-mission ask, not a storefront. Each line names what the money actually does, not just what you get.

Why it's laid out this way

  • Four tiers, all visible at once — the $75 tier is there to anchor, so $15 reads as modest by comparison, not because many people are expected to pick it.
  • Round numbers ($5/$15/$35/$75), not $4.99/$14.99 — charm pricing reads as "trying to extract a little more," which cuts against a support-the-mission ask.
  • No "Recommended" tag on any tier — all four sit at equal visual weight, so it's genuinely your pick, not a nudge toward one.
  • Each description says what the money funds, not just what's unlocked — purpose-based giving motivates more reliably than a transactional "get this" framing.
  • Nothing here implies the free app is missing anything — every tier description is about funding the mission, never about what you don't get otherwise.
  • Icons: Spark is a point of light (not the original lightning bolt — that read as electric, which fought the warmth/guidance story the other three names tell). Candle is a flame on a candle body — a bare flame alone read as a water droplet, not fire; the body gives it the context to be unambiguous, and it matches the name exactly. Lantern and Beacon are unchanged from the first pass — both already were exactly what their names say.
