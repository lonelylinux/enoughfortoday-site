How the Numbers Work

Drafted — style sample

Think of each day like a battery you wake up with. Enough for Today keeps track of how much you spend and how much you get back — so you don't have to do that math in your head.

Every task has two sides: what it costs you, and what it pays you back. A nap might cost nothing and pay back a lot. A big work task might cost more than it gives. The app tracks this across three kinds of energy — Physical, Emotional, and Cognitive — because a day can drain one of these a lot more than the others.

Each morning, you tell the app a few things about how you're doing — like your Body Battery, or how you slept. From that, it works out your Spendable Budget for the day: basically, how much you can safely take on.

Then, as you log what you actually do, the app keeps a running tally — what you've spent, what it's paid back, and what you have left. You don't have to track any of this yourself. Just log honestly, and the numbers take care of themselves.

Tour: Reports

Outline

Reports is one continuous scrolling page, not several tabs. In order: Budget adherence (Last 7 days / Last 30 days / All time, bar chart color-coded green/amber/red/grey, rest-day dots); Cost by dimension (stacked Physical/Emotional/Cognitive bars); Net trend (bars above/ below a zero line — trending toward recharge vs. depletion); Recovery inputs (Body Battery, sleep score, HRV strips to visually line up against the charts above); Day Mode calendar (month view, color legend); Menu insights (per-activity logged count and average cost/payoff/ net across history — this is where patterns about which tasks are actually worth it become visible).

Slack Time (React / Recreation)

Life interrupts. A phone call, a crisis, a last-minute ask — these things don't wait for a good time. If you haven't planned for them, they get paid for by borrowing from somewhere else in your day — usually rest.

That's what Slack Time is for. In your Default Day, there's a task called React / Recreation. It has a real cost and a real payoff — both set to 9 — so it holds space in your budget on purpose, without actually costing you anything net.

Here's the clever part: if something unplanned comes up, you edit that entry to say what really happened, and jot a note about it. If nothing comes up? That time is just yours — recreation, already budgeted for, no guilt required.

Either way, the rest of your day stays protected.
