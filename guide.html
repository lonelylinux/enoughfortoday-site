How the Numbers Work

Drafted — style sample

Everything in Enough for Today comes down to three pairs of numbers: Cost, Payoff, and the Net between them — tracked separately across three dimensions: Physical, Emotional, and Cognitive.

Every task you log has a Cost (what it takes out of you) and a Payoff (what it gives back), in each of those three dimensions. Net is just Payoff minus Cost — positive means the task recharged you more than it drained you; negative means it cost more than it gave. A nap might be Cost 0, Payoff 9 — pure recharge. A heavy work task might be Cost 8, Payoff 5 — necessary, but a real draw on the tank.

Your morning check-in (Body Battery, sleep score, HRV, and a few others) determines your Spendable Budget for the day — how much total Cost you can take on before you're pushing past what your body actually has available. As you log tasks, the app tracks Used, Payoff, and Remaining against that budget in real time, right on the Today screen.

You don't need to do this math yourself. The app does it live, every time you add, edit, or remove a task. Your job is just to log honestly — the numbers take care of the rest.

Tour: Reports

Outline

Reports is one continuous scrolling page, not several tabs. In order: Budget adherence (Last 7 days / Last 30 days / All time, bar chart color-coded green/amber/red/grey, rest-day dots); Cost by dimension (stacked Physical/Emotional/Cognitive bars); Net trend (bars above/ below a zero line — trending toward recharge vs. depletion); Recovery inputs (Body Battery, sleep score, HRV strips to visually line up against the charts above); Day Mode calendar (month view, color legend); Menu insights (per-activity logged count and average cost/payoff/ net across history — this is where patterns about which tasks are actually worth it become visible).

Slack Time (React / Recreation)

Outline — notes below for drafting

Unplanned demands don't ask permission — if nothing's budgeted for them, they get paid for by borrowing against whatever was planned. Scheduling the interruption in advance, as a real task with a real Cost, is what keeps it from cannibalizing the rest of the day. The twist: this isn't a line that just empties. React / Recreation is a real entry in your own Default Day, Cost 9 / Payoff 9 / Net +0 — it holds that battery space on purpose. If something unplanned shows up, you edit the day's copy of it and the notes field to reflect what actually happened. If nothing does, it's recreation time you'd never have scheduled on purpose. Either way, nothing gets silently borrowed from the rest of the day.
